# Summer 1 GRTQC experiment state

The immutable source is the 24.263-second TQC champion at `models/v2-dqn-qr-migrated-20260927/summer-1/tqc/champion/`. Its `policy.zip` SHA-256 is `ffbea4ca57116cd2586c17ccec4fc761600e0d1ee5c6d94e31b98122220daece`. The path's old name is historical; this migration does not load its former algorithm.

The transferred GRTQC checkpoint is `models/summer-1/grtqc/initialization/`. All eight source actor tensors were copied by name. Four new actor gate tensors have zero weights and biases; `2 × sigmoid(0) = 1`, so the actor gate is an exact identity at initialization while retaining a nonzero learning gradient. Fresh critic gates use ordinary sigmoid with zero weights and biases, giving an initially active 0.5 multiplier. Across 2,048 observations sampled from the source's saved Summer 1 replay, both maximum raw and post-overlay deterministic action errors were `0.0`. The source checkpoint was not changed. `transfer.json` records these measurements and the completed five-lap live validation.

An earlier near-pass-through gate with +15 bias differed by at most `2.4e-6` offline but failed all five live laps at about 53% progress. That diagnostic checkpoint is retained under `models/summer-1/grtqc/failed-initialization-20261001/`. A separate live evaluation confirmed the untouched TQC source still completed 5/5 laps at 24.263 seconds. The exact-identity gate is therefore required for this trajectory.

A second initialization transferred actor weights exactly but mistakenly replaced the source's nine speed-bias windows with an empty metadata field. The old TQC archive stores those windows inside `policy.zip`; its metadata does not repeat them. Paired laps then matched at 25.341 seconds, which is slower than the real 24.263-second source. That run was stopped before it could produce a champion. Its checkpoint and diagnostics are retained under `models/summer-1/grtqc/failed-initialization-missing-schedule-20261001/`. The transfer now copies the archive schedule and the live gate checks the reference's absolute median against 24.263 seconds as well as the candidate's lap delta.

The corrected transfer then passed 5/5 paired live laps at 24.263 seconds with zero action and lap-time delta. Its first 5,000-step evaluation also finished 5/5 at 24.263 seconds. The initial `1e-5` actor LR was too high: after critic warmup and actor unlock, 10k–25k evaluations completed 0/5. That run was stopped; its logs and rejected checkpoints remain. Even at `1e-7`, the first actor update produced 0/5 completions. On 8,192 saved observations, the first rejected checkpoint differed from initialization by at most 0.000113 in steering and 0.000366 in longitudinal action. Gate parameter updates account for much of that drift. The revised run gathers longer, slightly varied critic-only experience and allows three weaker evaluations before restoring a verified actor. The longer warmup still produced 0/5 on the first two actor evaluations and 5/5 at 25.419 seconds on the third, so the verified actor was restored. A local action-change limit of `1e-5` per optimizer step was then added for the next run; it scales back the proposed gradient step on that replay batch without prescribing an action or anchoring to the teacher. The first bounded-step run still failed its first unlocked evaluation. A regular evaluation coincided with unlock and an unintended duplicate check ran one decision later, so recovery fired after only 1,000 more decisions; the duplicate scheduling was fixed before continuing. This remains a controlled hypothesis, not a claim of a faster policy.

The first October 2 profile was `profiles/training/summer-1-grtqc-pace-30.json`, with a separate output root at `models/experiments/grtqc-pace-20261002/`. It uses the source's 128×128 actor/critic width, 30-tick frame skip, saved overlays and speed-bias schedule, 250,000-transition replay, batch 64, gamma 0.995, tau 0.005, one gradient update per four decisions, and automatic entropy initialized to 0.01. Actor and critic rates are `3e-6` and `5e-5`; the disagreement coefficient remains 0.01. It collects 4,500 decisions before updates, then requires at least 4,000 critic-only updates and a stable 200-update readiness window. Actor-frozen collection is deterministic so replay contains completed source laps. After unlock, exploration has standard deviation 0.006 and correlation 0.9 between decisions, resetting on episode completion. The deterministic actor step limit is `5e-5` on both the replay batch and representative verified-lap states. The permanent cumulative distance cap is disabled (`0`), allowing successive learned changes to depart from the initial policy.

Five-lap evaluations occur every 5,000 decisions while frozen and every 1,000 after unlock. Three consecutive unreliable or excessively slower evaluations restore the best verified actor, retaining critics and replay. Recovery clears actor optimizer moments and exploration state, retains the configured learning rate, and requires at least 1,000 further critic updates plus the readiness check. Reliable faster medians become champions. Reliable lower-contact policies within 1.5 seconds of the best are separate contact candidates in this profile, including before the first faster champion; they can become recovery sources. A promotion refreshes the representative guard states from its verified laps. No action or maneuver is prescribed by these mechanisms.

The actor warmup gate checks that the recent halves of both quantile loss and critic disagreement are finite and that the newer half has not increased by more than 10%. This is a stability heuristic, not a proof that the critic is accurate; the deterministic lap gate remains the performance test.

The publicly available [GRTQC article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2026.132517) describes gated actor/critic MLPs and an ensemble variance penalty on quantile estimates, but no public author code was located. PolyBot therefore records its transfer-preserving actor gate scale, critic gate initialization, reduction axes and coefficient explicitly. TQC's quantile truncation, soft target update, automatic entropy and replay update are retained from [SB3 Contrib TQC](https://sb3-contrib.readthedocs.io/en/master/modules/tqc.html).

Live transfer evaluation, critic warmup and GRTQC fine-tuning results are written to `transfer.json`, model metadata and the run JSONL log. Rejected checkpoints retain policy and metadata without copying the large replay buffer at every evaluation; the rolling `latest` checkpoint remains fully resumable. No sub-22 lap is claimed until a five-finish deterministic evaluation confirms it.

The transferred actor was also tested at frame skip 20 for five deterministic laps. It finished 5/5 at 24.616 seconds, 0.353 seconds slower than the same actor at frame skip 30. The 30-tick configuration therefore remains the primary baseline; the 20-tick result is recorded in `logs/grtqc-frame-skip-20-20261001.json` locally.

At 66,002 GRTQC decisions, no candidate had beaten 24.263 seconds. The source and current replay buffers were checked for identical observation/action schemas, frame skip, reward scale, reward semantics and normalized reward coefficients. The source has 25,384 transitions, including 28 with reward above 10, compared with 66,000 current transitions. `tools/seed_grtqc_replay.py` created a separate 91,384-transition checkpoint under `models/summer-1/grtqc/replay-seeded-20261001/`, leaving the source and `latest` intact. The actor state is bitwise identical to `latest`; critic-only warmup was reset to let the value model learn from the combined replay before another actor update. `replay-source.json` records counts and hashes. This intervention is an experiment, not an achieved pace gain.

The wall contacts were then traced to a disabled reward threshold in that profile: `barrier_collision_impulse_threshold = 1e9` made the `-50` contact term unreachable. Setting the threshold to 500 exposed an additional classification bug: touchdown impulses continued for several decisions after the first grounded one. The detector now excludes the entire existing landing-grace window. Five live laps of the immutable source at frame skip 30 still finished at 24.263 seconds; each recorded exactly two non-landing contacts, at 9.32% and 99.22% progress. The separate `profiles/training/summer-1-grtqc-contact.json` run uses a nonterminal `-300` raw contact penalty (scaled by the existing 0.01 reward scale) and threshold 500. It starts from a new exact actor transfer with fresh replay because the older replay was collected under different reward coefficients. Neither contact location has a prescribed action or target line.

The first contact-aware 30-tick actor update still failed 5/5 despite a maximum deterministic action drift of only `3.6e-6` on 8,192 saved observations; rollback retained the 24.263-second initialization. The rejected actor was evaluated at frame skip 20 and finished 5/5 at 27.450 seconds, albeit with three contacts per lap. This suggests a 20-tick controller may tolerate policy changes better, though it does not show a pace improvement. `profiles/training/summer-1-grtqc-contact-20.json` is a separate experiment with a measured 24.616-second source baseline, unchanged contact reward and action schema, time-adjusted gamma `0.9966638827`, 4,500 initial replay decisions, one gradient update per four decisions, and 15,000 critic-only updates. It preserves the 30-tick checkpoint and requires its own five-lap transfer gate before RL updates.

The 20-tick transfer gate passed 5/5 live laps at 24.616 seconds with zero action and lap-time disagreement. Each lap registered four contact events (two near 9.3%, one near 55.5%, and one near 99.1%), so this setting is an exploration experiment rather than an already cleaner driver. The actor remains frozen while its critics adapt; only later deterministic evaluations can establish whether it removes contacts and improves pace.

## October 2 blocker audit

The source-faithful 30-tick run was stopped cleanly at 67,518 decisions and saved with replay. It did not beat 24.263 seconds. Actor unlock at approximately 30,517 decisions produced three failed evaluations, followed by actor recovery; a second unlock at approximately 59,518 repeated the failure. Frozen evaluations still completed 5/5 at 24.263 seconds with two non-landing contacts per lap. The prior recovery logic halved actor LR twice, from `1e-7` to `2.5e-8`, and repeated all 6,500 warmup updates despite retaining the trained critics. The first-chicane curriculum had already ended before either actor unlock. These observations support changing the learning schedule rather than running that setup longer.

The revised curriculum spends 15,000 decisions on full laps, 20,000 on the 7%–12% first-chicane section with a 5% lead-in, then 1,965,000 on full laps. The minimum critic warmup now overlaps that section, although readiness can delay unlock. Section bounds determine exposure and termination only, never desired controls. Contact rewards remain nonterminal `-300` raw points with impulse threshold 250 and landing filtering; ghost reward scale remains zero. Entropy, exploration and step limits are experimental hypotheses, not evidence that this run is faster.

The audit also found that the first cleaner policy could not be retained until a faster GRTQC champion existed. Initialization evaluation now supplies that comparison, and reliable contact candidates can be restored without a champion. Stale simulator episode errors are retried once during evaluation; persistent stale episodes and transport failures save the learner before propagating. The optional stop-file launcher reconnects from that saved learner and replay, preserving the remaining budget and curriculum position. An interrupted transfer validation repeats all five paired laps, preventing a retry from bypassing the fidelity gate.

The new live transfer completed 5/5 at 24.263 seconds with zero deterministic action and lap-time disagreement. Before its first gradient update, a further audit measured the transferred Gaussian policy's standard deviation: median approximately 0.406/0.412 before tanh, with extrema 0.141–1.123 across 2,048 replay observations. Critic targets and actor optimization had used this broad distribution while rollout collection was nearly deterministic. This substantial mismatch can require fresh critics to extrapolate to unsupported controls. The revised profile smoothly bounds the training distribution to 0.02 before tanh (`sigma = limit * sigma / (limit + sigma)`), retaining deterministic means, learned variance gradients and consistent log probabilities. Automatic entropy targets -10 rather than the default -2 so its objective is compatible with restricted variance. These are transfer fine-tuning choices; the default bound is disabled for other profiles.

Evaluation also forwarded the saved air-brake overlay's touchdown-release request to the environment, while GRTQC rollout collection did not. Training now forwards the same existing request through the wrapped vector environment before stepping, including the underlying exploration perturbation. This restores the source's control execution semantics rather than adding a maneuver. The initial collection was stopped at 3,400 decisions with no gradient updates; its replay remains archived locally and is not reused because it predates this execution fix. A fresh collection repeats the live fidelity gate before training.

Section boundaries were also marked as true task termination, dropping continuation value from off-policy targets at an artificial boundary. Sections now truncate collection and retain bootstrap from the real terminal observation; actual finishes and failures still terminate. This makes the critic's task consistent when section and full-lap transitions share replay. Existing replay containing sections recorded with true-terminal masks should be recollected; full-lap-only replay is unaffected.

After these fixes, full-lap collection completed 17 attempts without a crash. The actor unlocked at approximately 20,510 decisions. Its first two full-lap evaluations failed, but the 22,510-decision policy completed 5/5 laps at 25.359 seconds with one contact per lap (at 9.51% progress) and no terminal barrier contact. This is slower than the source and is not a champion. The previous 0.5-second contact-candidate tolerance rejected it, followed by recovery to the barrier-dependent source. The profile now allows a separate cleaner stepping-stone up to 1.5 seconds slower, based on this measured result, while keeping the faster-champion threshold unchanged. The already verified 22,510 checkpoint is retained as the contact candidate; continuation restores its actor while preserving the current critics and replay. Further speed gains remain unproven.

## Finish-credit correction

Subsequent updates repeatedly failed full laps, including after recovery to the cleaner policy. An audit of the first saved 809-decision source lap found a mean scaled driving reward of 0.050 per decision, while the entropy deduction averaged 0.081. At gamma 0.995, its discounted driving return was -1.923 and the entropy deduction was 17.836. The direct actor entropy gradient was only 11%–14% of the value-gradient norm in three replay samples; the larger problem was its accumulated contribution inside bootstrapped values, together with finish credit discounted to about 1.7% at the end of a 24.263-second lap.

There was also a reward bug: `barrier_contact` still counted as an incomplete failure after contacts became nonterminal. Every contact therefore charged progress clawback and early-failure costs in addition to its explicit impact cost. These extra costs now apply only to actual crash, off-track, stall, airborne-roll failure or timeout. Reward semantics are `nonterminal-contact-v2`, and TQC/GRTQC cannot reuse replay with the earlier semantics. The immutable source policy and all measured lap times remain intact; old replay is preserved locally rather than mixed into the corrected learner.

The current profile is `profiles/training/summer-1-grtqc-finish-credit-30.json`, with a separate output root at `models/experiments/grtqc-finish-credit-20261002/`. It retains the architecture, 30-tick controls, batch 64, four-decision update frequency, action guards and five-lap gates. Gamma is 0.999, giving approximately 30 seconds of discounted horizon and about 44.5% finish weight at 809 decisions. Automatic entropy starts at 0.0001 rather than 0.01, with target entropy -10 and the same smooth 0.02 standard-deviation bound. Explicit entropy initialization changes now take effect when resuming, while unchanged configurations retain their learned coefficient; actor recovery does not import a coefficient from a differently configured source.

The explicit nonterminal contact cost is -600 raw points. It is separate from true-failure penalties and is intended to make removing contacts worthwhile even if the first reliable alternative is slightly slower. The profile trains full laps. Initial replay fill remains deterministic; after that, 25% of critic-only episodes use correlated 0.0005 action noise so the critics see nearby alternatives as well as reliable complete laps. This exposes control/value differences without prescribing an action or line. The cleaner 25.359-second actor supplies continuation skill with fresh critics and replay under the corrected objective; it is still not a faster champion. Improvement must be established by subsequent live lap evaluations.

## Multi-step credit experiment

The corrected reward run preserved five-lap completion at 25.359 seconds while frozen, but its first unlocked evaluations failed or slowed to 27.630 and 28.969 seconds. A training lap reached 24.752 seconds; this was exploratory behavior, not a confirmed deterministic candidate. Subsequent failed evaluations restored the cleaner actor. The original 24.263-second source remains unbeaten.

Critic loss stability did not establish useful value calibration. At 10,000 decisions and 1,375 critic updates, the mean estimated value on completed laps was 0.302 versus 52.752 observed discounted driving return. A controlled offline comparison used identical 28,550-decision critic weights, the same 28,548-transition replay, a fixed random seed, the restored cleaner actor, and 2,000 critic-only updates per case. Mean absolute error against the first completed lap's observed return was 50.800 with one-step targets and 23.671 with 64-step targets; mean predicted values were 1.951 and 29.103. The actor stayed bitwise unchanged in both cases. Observed driving returns omit entropy and follow recorded behavior, so this is a calibration diagnostic rather than an exact soft-Q correctness test or evidence of faster laps.

The primary profile now uses 64-decision returns (up to 1.92 seconds at 30 ticks). This allows actual finish and contact rewards to influence more preceding states per update. SB3's multi-step replay supplies the accumulated discounted reward, corresponding final observation and actual bootstrap discount; GRTQC retains quantile truncation, gating, disagreement regularization and its actor update. Existing raw replay can be reused when changing horizon, with critic warmup restarted for the new targets. Replay storage arrays are reused without a second large allocation.

Evaluation and curriculum switches can reset the simulator between stored transitions without a true failure. These resets are now marked as truncations so multi-step sampling cannot combine two different episodes. Resuming older one-step replay also repairs such boundaries by comparing each transition's next observation with the following observation. True finishes and failures still suppress bootstrap. Tests check both terminal rewards and interrupted-episode bootstrap, along with lossless replay and actor preservation on migration.

Cleaner-candidate promotion also accepts a faster five-finish policy with the same reduced contact count. Previously it required strictly fewer contacts even after a cleaner candidate existed, which could discard gradual pace improvements. A slower or higher-contact policy cannot replace that candidate, and the champion threshold remains the verified source time.

Live migration retained 34,548 replay transitions at 34,551 decisions. After 1,250 multi-step updates, the completed-lap value estimate averaged 23.255 versus observed return 52.605, with mean absolute error 29.356; the immediately preceding one-step snapshot averaged 1.827 versus 52.580, with error 50.753. Frozen five-lap checks remained 25.359 seconds. Three subsequent actor-learning cycles still regressed and recovered; one evaluation finished 5/5 at 25.857 seconds, which is slower than the cleaner candidate. Better value calibration has therefore not yet established reliable policy improvement, and 24.263 seconds remains unbeaten.

An offline control-sensitivity check compared the first rejected multi-step update with the cleaner policy on identical recorded lap observations. Maximum requested steering and longitudinal differences were approximately `5.23e-5` and `2.48e-5`. Nominal 30-tick PWM schedules differed in 680 tick positions across 248 decisions. The first timing difference occurred at 3.88% progress with only `1.35e-5` action difference, before the early contact. This excludes touchdown switching and is not a simulated counterfactual lap; it demonstrates pulse-timing sensitivity, without proving which changed pulse caused the live failure. Tiny continuous action guards alone do not ensure preserved digital execution or lap completion.

Fresh-replay continuation also explicitly refreezes the actor and clears critic-readiness history, even when the return horizon is unchanged. This prevents a model that previously unlocked from immediately updating against newly collected reward data after a reward change.

## Pulse feedback and evaluation spacing

The multi-step learner was stopped cleanly at 75,589 decisions for diagnostics. A new five-lap 20-tick comparison again reproduced the immutable source at 24.616 seconds, but both the 25.359-second cleaner policy and the first rejected multi-step update failed all five laps near 23.4% progress. More frequent control is therefore not established as a solution for these candidates; the primary configuration retains 30 ticks.

The observation includes the last digital steering, throttle and brake flags at indices 42–44, while physical steering is separately represented at index 33. On the cleaner policy's recorded lap, changing only the last steering flag to another valid flag produced substantial next-action changes. Comparing the first rejected update's nominal PWM schedule against the cleaner schedule found 22 changed final-tick flags, all matching the corresponding recorded baseline flags. Counterfactual next-action changes reached 0.166 in steering and 0.290 in longitudinal demand. The first such change was at 10.77% progress; none occurred before the first contact. This is evidence of inherited feedback sensitivity, not proof that this mechanism caused the early contact or every subsequent failure.

A live diagnostic changed only that first steering flag once at decision 138, leaving subsequent decisions to the unmodified policy. It finished in 25.041 seconds versus the 25.359-second control lap. This was an input intervention for diagnosis, not a learned improvement or a deployed driving rule. No prescribed action or section maneuver was added.

A controlled off-policy comparison restored the cleaner actor onto identical 75,589-decision learner weights and replay, reset actor optimizer moments, and used random seed 17. The ordinary GRTQC objective and two experimental penalties on previous-steering sensitivity all failed their first-update five-lap checks. After 128 updates, ordinary GRTQC completed 5/5 at 24.491 seconds with two contacts per lap. Both sensitivity-penalty variants still failed all five; that penalty was removed rather than enabled in the trainer. The 24.491-second actor is retained as a verified continuation experiment, not a champion: it remains slower than the immutable 24.263-second source and has more contacts than the cleaner candidate.

The successful intermediate policy motivates denser evaluation, without proving that every learning trajectory has a useful intermediate policy. The primary profile now checks every 512 decisions while the actor is unlocked, using a one-lap screen to avoid spending five laps on an already failed policy. Every finishing screen is followed by a separate full five-lap evaluation; only that result can promote a candidate or confirm the target. Five consecutive weak checks trigger recovery, preserving approximately the previous 2,000-decision opportunity between the first weak check and rollback. Defaults retain the prior 1,000-decision spacing, three weak checks and full evaluation, and the settings round-trip through the GUI.

Dense checks initially interrupted full-track collection sooner than a complete lap could finish. The profile now holds the actor when a check is due and finishes the current attempt before resetting for evaluation; critic updates and replay collection continue during that hold. Readable status distinguishes this brief hold from initial critic warmup. Pending collection ends through the regular rollout loop, so SB3 stores the finish/failure transition before evaluation. If a curriculum phase or total budget ends first, the check may proceed at that boundary. During critic warmup, smaller collection chunks notice actor unlock without aborting inside the callback before the transition is stored. Resuming clears a temporary evaluation hold.

Live continuation from the verified 24.491-second actor resumed at 83,687 decisions with retained critics and replay. The handoff allowed a 27.346-second exploratory finish to enter training before evaluation; it was not a verified pace result. At 86,599 decisions, a subsequent actor completed all five deterministic evaluation laps in 24.645 seconds, with exactly one contact per lap at 9.60% progress and no terminal contact. It replaced the 25.359-second cleaner candidate for the same contact count and a 0.714-second faster median. The saved `contact-candidate/` includes replay and remains available for recovery. This is a confirmed improvement on the cleaner branch, not a faster champion: the immutable two-contact 24.263-second TQC source remains unbeaten, and repeated actor regressions are not yet resolved.

## Critic controller-state experiment

Further learning continued to alternate failed screens with slower complete evaluations; it did not beat 24.263 seconds. A deterministic control test established a missing-state alias: 30 ticks requested at steering 0.01 and 0.03 both produce no digital steering pulses, leaving identical physical observations and previous digital flags. Their hidden PWM accumulators differ, approximately 0.3 and 0.9. The same subsequent request, steering 0.02 for 30 ticks, produces zero pulses in the first case and one in the second. The previous critic therefore received the same observation and action for different control executions, even before considering trajectory divergence.

The separate `profiles/training/summer-1-grtqc-controller-state-30.json` experiment adds the two PWM accumulators and their directions to critic observations. The actor extracts the unchanged original observation prefix, preserving the exact source architecture, weights and driving behavior. With 12 lookahead points, the actor uses 105 inputs and the critics use 109. The appended values come from the existing controller and do not change its scheduling or prescribe an action. This resolves the demonstrated controller alias; it does not establish full observability of every simulator/reward state or prove that policy regressions are solved.

The layout is named `polybot.observation.v2.pwm-state`. Metadata validation rejects mixing it with legacy observations and replay, even for otherwise matching tracks and algorithms. The new experiment uses fresh critics and replay under `models/experiments/grtqc-controller-state-20261002/`; the prior learner and its replay remain intact. Existing profiles retain legacy inputs by default. Tests verify identical physical prefixes with different controller states, phase reset, critic access to those inputs, exact source actor outputs independent of the suffix, model save/load, working critic updates, schema rejection, and GUI round-trip.

Offline transfer reproduced all actions on 2,048 saved source observations with zero error. Live validation then completed five paired source/student laps at exactly 24.263 seconds with zero action and time difference. The prior 24.645-second one-contact actor was also converted without changing its original weights or controls, verified over five live laps at 24.645 seconds, and retained as the compatible cleaner recovery candidate with empty fresh replay. No legacy transitions were padded or reused for critic training. The new learner starts from the exact 24.263-second TQC graft and collects controller-state replay before actor updates. Other reward and training settings remain those of the finish-credit profile, allowing later results to test this state representation rather than an unrelated parameter change. The confirmed sub-22 objective remains unmet.

Frozen evaluations at 5,000, 10,000, 15,000 and 20,000 decisions retained five finishes at 24.263 seconds. The first unlocked cycle still produced five failed screens and recovered at 24,160 decisions to the compatible cleaner actor. Thus controller state fixes the demonstrated input alias but has not, by itself, solved unstable policy improvement. Further experiments must test the value-guided update and its training distribution rather than interpreting successful transfer as progress on lap time.
